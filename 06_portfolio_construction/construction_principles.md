# Construction Principles - Meridian Ventures Fund I

## The Discipline Layer

Portfolio construction principles exist to prevent
three failure modes that systematically destroy
venture fund returns: concentration risk from
oversized positions in single companies, corridor
risk from geographic overexposure, and style drift
from deploying outside the mandate when competitive
pressure makes on-mandate deals harder to source.

Each principle below is a rule, not a guideline.
Rules have bright lines. Guidelines have exceptions
that accumulate until the original intent is lost.

---

## Principle 1 - Position Sizing

**Rule:** No single position may exceed 15% of total
fund commitments at cost across initial investment
and follow-on combined.

At USD 200M fund size, the maximum single position
is USD 30M across initial and follow-on tranches.
A position at maximum size represents a 15%
concentration that, if written off entirely, produces
a 15% drag on total fund capital before any other
position contributes to returns.

**Why 15% and not 10% or 20%:**
10% would limit maximum positions to USD 20M, which
constrains the fund's ability to lead Series A rounds
in the USD 30M to 50M valuation range where the
strongest Asia-Pacific wealthtech companies are
currently priced. 20% would allow a concentration
that creates excessive sensitivity to single-position
outcomes, reducing the diversification benefit of
a 10 to 15 position portfolio to near zero in
adverse scenarios.

**Follow-on application:**
The 15% limit applies to combined initial and
follow-on deployment. A USD 10M initial investment
has USD 20M of follow-on capacity before hitting
the limit. In practice, follow-on deployment is
governed by the Stage Gate 2 criteria before the
position size limit becomes binding.

## Principle 2 - Geographic Diversification

**Rule:** No single corridor may represent more than
40% of total deployed capital at cost.

The five corridors - China, Singapore, Indonesia,
Macau, Brunei - are exposed to different regulatory,
currency, and political risks. A portfolio concentrated
in a single corridor has taken on corridor risk
that is not compensated by return differential
and that cannot be diversified away within the fund.

**Corridor minimum:**
The fund does not set a minimum allocation per
corridor. Brunei and Macau will naturally represent
smaller allocations given the smaller absolute
deal flow volume in those corridors. What the
fund does require is that the GP actively sources
across all five corridors throughout the deployment
period, not just in the corridors where deal flow
is most readily available.

**Secondary corridors:**
Deals sourced from secondary corridors outside
the five primary corridors are permitted up to
10% of total deployed capital where the GP documents
a specific private banking client network sourcing
advantage. Secondary corridor exposure requires
LP Advisory Board notification at the time of IC approval.

## Principle 3 - Stage Discipline

**Rule:** Minimum 60% of deployed capital at cost
must be in Series A positions. Maximum 40% may be
in Series B positions including follow-on participation.

This rule preserves the uncertainty-reduction
arbitrage window as the fund's primary return source.
A fund that drifts toward Series B deployment is
chasing execution evidence that is already partially
priced. The return compression at Series B relative
to Series A reflects the information advantage
that earlier investors have already monetized.

**Follow-on classification:**
Series B follow-on in existing Series A portfolio
companies is classified as Series B deployment for
the purpose of this rule. A USD 10M initial Series A
investment followed by USD 5M Series B follow-on
contributes USD 5M to the Series B allocation bucket,
not USD 15M to the Series A bucket.

**Exception process:**
Deviation from the 60/40 stage ratio requires
LP Advisory Board approval. The IC may not unilaterally
approve a portfolio composition that breaches the
stage discipline rule even temporarily.

## Principle 4 - Sector Concentration

**Rule:** No single sector may represent more than
35% of deployed capital at cost.

The five focus sectors - wealthtech, cross-border
payment, family office platforms, Islamic finance
technology, real estate analytics - have different
regulatory risk profiles, competitive dynamics,
and exit timing characteristics. Concentration in
a single sector creates correlated risk that is
not offset by geographic diversification.

**Wealthtech concentration risk:**
Wealthtech is the largest opportunity set within
Meridian's mandate and the sector where the private
banking intelligence advantage is strongest. There
is a natural pull toward overweighting wealthtech.
The 35% limit prevents this pull from creating
a portfolio that is essentially a sector fund
operating under a multi-sector mandate.

**Sector classification:**
Companies that operate across multiple sectors
are classified in their primary revenue-generating
sector at the time of initial investment. Classification
is reviewed annually and may be updated in the
IC decision log if the company's revenue mix
shifts materially.

## Principle 5 - Co-Investment Allocation

**Rule:** Maximum 20% of any single deal may be
offered as co-investment combined across LP advisory
board members and Meridian private banking clients.

Co-investment creates relationship value for Meridian's
private banking clients and strengthens the fund's
LP relationships when structured appropriately.
It creates misaligned incentives when it is structured
in a way that competes with the fund's own deployment.

**Client co-investment priority:**
Within the 20% co-investment allocation, Meridian's
VHNW and UHNW private banking clients with
co_investment_eligible_flag set to TRUE receive
priority allocation. LP advisory board co-investment
rights are exercised from the remaining allocation
after client allocations are filled.

**Minimum ticket size:**
Client co-investment minimum ticket is USD 250K.
LP advisory board co-investment minimum ticket
is USD 1M. These minimums ensure that co-investment
administration costs are proportionate to the
relationship value created.

## Principle 6 - Follow-On Reserve Discipline

**Rule:** 40% of total fund commitments are reserved
for follow-on deployment. Follow-on reserve is not
deployed in new positions.

The follow-on reserve exists to fund Stage Gate 2
participation in the strongest Series A performers.
Deploying reserve capital in new positions instead
of following on in existing winners is a form of
portfolio construction failure: it signals that
the initial portfolio did not generate enough
conviction for follow-on deployment and that the
GP is recycling capital into new bets rather than
compounding winners.

**Reserve release:**
Follow-on reserve capital that is not deployed
by year 6 is returned to LPs as a distribution
rather than deployed in new positions outside
the mandate. Undeployed follow-on capital is not
a reason to lower investment standards in late-fund
deployment.

---

## Principle 7 - No Style Drift

**Rule:** All investments must fall within the
five sectors and five corridors defined in the
blind pool thesis. Geographic and sector exceptions
require LP Advisory Board approval.

Style drift is the gradual departure from the
investment mandate that occurs when on-mandate
deal flow is insufficient to fill the deployment
schedule. The GP responds by approving deals that
are adjacent to the mandate, then deals that are
further from it, until the portfolio bears no
resemblance to what LPs committed to.

The rule against style drift is enforced by requiring
LP Advisory Board approval for any exception rather
than by relying on IC discipline alone. External
governance is more reliable than self-governance
under deployment pressure.
