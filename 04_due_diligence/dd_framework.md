# Due Diligence Framework - Meridian Ventures

## Five Dimensions

Every potential investment is assessed across five
dimensions before IC submission. Each dimension
receives a pass, flag, or fail assessment. The
assessment is not a score - it is a judgment about
whether the evidence in that dimension supports
the investment thesis or raises concerns that require
resolution before capital is deployed.

A pass means the evidence in this dimension is
consistent with the investment thesis and does not
require further investigation before the IC vote.

A flag means the evidence in this dimension raises
a concern that has been identified, investigated,
and documented, but does not individually disqualify
the investment. Flags must be accompanied by a
monitoring plan that defines what observation would
escalate the flag to a fail post-investment.

A fail means the evidence in this dimension is
inconsistent with the investment thesis in a way
that cannot be resolved through additional information
or contractual protections. A single fail assessment
requires explicit IC discussion. Two or more fail
assessments typically result in rejection.

## Dimension 1 - Business Model

**What is being assessed:** The structural soundness
of the revenue model, the quality of the unit economics,
and the credibility of the path from current financial
position to the exit scenario implied by the P50
LHS valuation case.

**Analytical approach:**

Revenue quality assessment. Every revenue stream
is decomposed into its customer, contract, and
cohort components. Recurring revenue is separated
from transactional revenue. Contracted revenue is
separated from usage-based revenue. Cohort analysis
identifies whether early customers are expanding,
stable, or contracting - the single most informative
signal of product-market fit quality.

Unit economics construction. The IC team builds
an independent unit economics model from first
principles using data provided during DD. Customer
acquisition cost is calculated from actual sales
and marketing spend divided by new customers
acquired, not from founder-provided CAC figures.
Lifetime value is calculated from observed cohort
retention, not from projected retention.

Margin trajectory analysis. Gross margin at current
revenue scale is documented. The structural gross
margin at scale - the gross margin the business
model produces when variable costs are fully
optimized - is estimated and compared to sector
benchmarks. The gap between current gross margin
and structural gross margin is a primary driver
of the exit multiple assumption in the LHS model.

**Pass criteria:** Revenue predominantly recurring
and contracted. Gross margin positive or on documented
trajectory. Unit economics calculable with available
data. Path to structural gross margin consistent
with exit multiple assumptions.

**Flag criteria:** Revenue mix shifting toward
lower-quality transactional sources. Gross margin
improvement dependent on cost reductions not yet
contracted. CAC increasing faster than LTV.

**Fail criteria:** Revenue generated primarily from
a single customer representing more than 40% of
total. Negative gross margin with no credible path
to positivity within 18 months. Unit economics
that do not close at any realistic scale.

## Dimension 2 - Team Execution

**What is being assessed:** Whether the founding team
has demonstrated the operational discipline, judgment
under pressure, and organizational capability that
characterizes exceptional execution rather than
favorable early circumstances.

**Analytical approach:**

Operational track record reconstruction. For each
key milestone the company claims to have achieved,
the DD team independently verifies the timeline,
the resource consumption required, and whether the
outcome was achieved through systematic capability
or through a specific advantage that may not recur.

Hiring and retention analysis. The composition of
the current team is mapped against the operational
requirements of the next 18 months. Key person
dependencies are identified. Attrition history is
reviewed. Companies that have retained strong
operational talent through a difficult period have
demonstrated something about their culture and
leadership that companies with low attrition in a
benign period have not.

Founder-market fit evaluation. The founding team's
prior experience is mapped against the specific
operational challenges of the target market. Relevant
experience in Asia-Pacific financial services,
technology, or the target sector is a positive signal.
Its absence is not automatically disqualifying but
requires compensating evidence of market learning speed.

Reference network activation. Meridian's private
banking client network across five Asia-Pacific corridors
is activated to provide ground-level intelligence
about the founding team's reputation, operational
approach, and client relationships in the relevant
markets. References are sought from customers,
former employees, co-investors, and industry
participants - not solely from references provided
by the founders.

**Pass criteria:** Track record of milestone achievement
consistent with resource plan. Key talent retention
evidence across minimum 12-month operating history.
Founder-market fit evidenced by prior operational
experience or demonstrated market learning. Positive
independent reference feedback.

**Flag criteria:** Single founder dependency with no
succession plan. Recent key hire departures not
proactively disclosed. References that are warm but
lack specific operational evidence.

**Fail criteria:** Material misrepresentation of
operational achievements during DD. Unresolved
conflict between founding team members that has
affected operational decision-making. Multiple
negative independent references from customers
or former employees.

## Dimension 3 - Market

**What is being assessed:** Whether the market
opportunity is large enough to support the exit
scenario in the P50 LHS valuation case, and whether
the competitive dynamics of the market allow a
well-executed company to capture a meaningful share.

**Analytical approach:**

Independent market sizing. The DD team constructs
a bottom-up market size estimate independent of
the founder's TAM claims. Bottom-up estimation
starts from the number of identifiable potential
customers in the five Asia-Pacific corridors,
multiplies by average contract value at maturity,
and adjusts for realistic penetration rates given
the competitive environment. This independent
estimate is compared to the founder's TAM and
the discrepancy is investigated.

Competitive landscape mapping. The three to five
most credible competitive alternatives are identified
and their current market position, funding status,
and strategic trajectory are documented. The question
being answered is not whether the target company
is better than competitors today, but whether its
competitive position is defensible as better-resourced
incumbents or later entrants respond to its growth.

Regulatory environment assessment. For each of the
five Asia-Pacific corridors where the target company
operates or plans to operate, the relevant regulatory
requirements are documented and the company's current
compliance posture is assessed. Regulatory risk is
particularly acute in Singapore MAS-regulated fintech,
Indonesian OJK-regulated financial services, and
Shariah certification requirements for Islamic finance
products.

**Pass criteria:** Independent market size estimate
above USD 1B within the five corridors. Competitive
differentiation that is defensible for the 18 to
24 months required to reach the next financing milestone.
Regulatory compliance posture appropriate for current
operational stage.

**Flag criteria:** Market size dependent on optimistic
penetration assumptions not supported by comparable
market development trajectories. A well-funded competitor
entering the market within the DD period. Regulatory
compliance gap that requires remediation before
the next financing milestone.

**Fail criteria:** Independent market size estimate
below USD 500M within the five corridors. A dominant
incumbent with the resources to replicate the target
company's product within 12 months and the distribution
to deploy it at scale. Regulatory non-compliance
that creates material liability risk.

## Dimension 4 - Financial

**What is being assessed:** The quality, reliability,
and analytical depth of the company's financial
information, and whether the financial position
supports the operational plan through to the next
financing milestone.

**Analytical approach:**

Financial statement review. Audited or reviewed
financial statements for all available periods are
analyzed. The DD team focuses on revenue recognition
policies, deferred revenue treatment, related party
transactions, and the consistency of financial
reporting across periods. Inconsistencies in accounting
treatment between periods are investigated.

Cash runway calculation. The company's current cash
position is divided by the trailing three-month average
monthly burn rate to produce an independent runway
estimate. This estimate is compared to the founder's
runway claim and to the timeline required to reach
the next financing milestone. Runway below nine months
at the time of IC submission without a committed
follow-on financing plan is a flag.

Financial model review. The company's financial model
is assessed for internal consistency, the
reasonableness of growth assumptions relative to
operating history, and whether the operational
cost structure scales appropriately with revenue.
Financial models that assume significant cost
leverage without identifying the specific operational
changes that produce it are flagged.

Cap table analysis. The full capitalization table
is reviewed including all outstanding options,
warrants, convertible instruments, and side letters.
The post-money cap table implied by the proposed
Series A terms is constructed to confirm Meridian's
ownership percentage and preference stack position.

**Pass criteria:** Financial statements consistent
with operational claims made during DD. Cash runway
above nine months to next financing milestone.
Financial model internally consistent with
documented assumptions. Cap table clean with no
undisclosed instruments.

**Flag criteria:** Runway between six and nine months
with a financing plan in process but not committed.
Financial model assumptions that are aggressive
relative to comparable company operating histories.
Minor cap table inconsistencies that are resolvable
before close.

**Fail criteria:** Runway below six months with
no committed financing plan. Material inconsistency
between financial statements and operational claims.
Undisclosed instruments on the cap table that
materially affect Meridian's preference stack position.

## Dimension 5 - Strategic Fit

**What is being assessed:** Whether the investment
creates value for Meridian beyond the financial
return from the position itself, through deal sourcing
network effects, co-investment distribution, private
banking client intelligence, or brand positioning.

**Analytical approach:**

Co-investment suitability assessment. The eligibility
of the investment for co-investment offering to
Meridian's VHNW and UHNW client base is assessed
against three criteria: client network familiarity
with the sector, minimum co-investment ticket size
compatibility with the client base, and absence
of conflicts between the investment's risk profile
and the suitability requirements of the clients
who would be offered the co-investment.

Deal sourcing network value. The investment is assessed
for its potential to generate future deal flow through
the portfolio company's network. A portfolio company
whose founders have deep networks in the target sector
and the five Asia-Pacific corridors is a more valuable
member of the Meridian portfolio than an equally
strong company with a narrower network footprint.

Private banking intelligence value. The investment
is assessed for its potential to generate market
intelligence that improves Meridian's private banking
client service. A wealthtech portfolio company whose
product Meridian's private banking clients use
generates continuous intelligence about client
needs and market developments that a consumer
technology investment does not.

**Pass criteria:** Investment suitable for co-investment
offering to minimum 10% of co-investment eligible
client base. Founding team network adds to Meridian's
deal sourcing coverage in at least one of the five
sectors or five corridors. Product or market relevance
to Meridian's private banking client base documented.

**Flag criteria:** Co-investment suitability limited
to UHNW tier only due to minimum ticket size.
Founding team network limited to a single corridor
with no cross-corridor presence.

**Fail criteria:** Investment in a sector or geography
with no connection to Meridian's private banking
client base and no potential co-investment distribution.
An investment that meets financial criteria but
provides no strategic value beyond the return
is a legitimate alternative investment for a
generalist fund. Meridian deploys capital where
strategic fit amplifies financial returns.
