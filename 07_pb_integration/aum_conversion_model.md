# AUM Conversion Model - Meridian Ventures

## The Flywheel

The AUM conversion model describes the financial
and relationship logic through which venture
co-investment events compound into sustained AUM
growth for Meridian's private banking business.
It is not a model in the quantitative sense.
It is a framework for understanding how the
private banking and venture arms of the institution
reinforce each other across the investment lifecycle.

---

## Stage 1 - Pre-Investment: Co-Investment as Portfolio Allocation

When a VHNW or UHNW client makes a co-investment
alongside Meridian Ventures, the co-investment
position enters the client's portfolio as an
illiquid alternative allocation. For the duration
of the holding period, this allocation:

Increases the client's total investable assets
under Meridian's advisory or discretionary mandate.
A client with USD 10M in a discretionary mandate
who co-invests USD 500K has USD 10.5M in total
Meridian-managed assets. Management fee revenue
increases proportionally.

Deepens the client's relationship with Meridian
beyond the standard portfolio management interaction.
The client now has a direct financial stake in a
Meridian investment decision. They are no longer
a passive recipient of portfolio management services.
They are a co-investor with shared upside and a
genuine interest in the portfolio company's performance.

Creates a recurring touchpoint beyond the standard
quarterly portfolio review. Portfolio company
updates, milestone announcements, and market
developments relevant to the co-investment are
legitimate reasons for the relationship manager
to initiate contact between standard review cycles.
Each of these touchpoints is an opportunity to
deepen the relationship and identify additional
wealth management needs.

---

## Stage 2 - Holding Period: Intelligence and Relationship Deepening

During the holding period, the co-investment
relationship generates intelligence and relationship
depth that the standard advisory or discretionary
mandate does not.

**Intelligence generation:**
A client who has co-invested in a wealthtech
infrastructure company is a more engaged source
of market intelligence about that sector than
a client whose exposure is purely through a
fund allocation. When the relationship manager
asks for the client's perspective on competitive
developments in the wealthtech sector, the
co-invested client has a personal financial
incentive to share what they know. This intelligence
feeds back into Meridian Ventures' portfolio
monitoring and, potentially, into the sourcing
pipeline for future investments.

**Relationship depth:**
A client relationship that includes a shared
investment decision, shared upside exposure,
and ongoing co-investor communication is
structurally more durable than a client relationship
based solely on portfolio management services.
The co-investment creates a partnership dimension
that institutional competitors without a venture
arm cannot offer.

**Referral probability:**
Clients with co-investment relationships refer
new clients at higher rates than clients without
co-investment relationships. The referral is
more specific and more persuasive than a general
recommendation: "Meridian manages my portfolio
and I co-invested alongside them in a venture
company that is doing well" is a more compelling
referral than "Meridian manages my portfolio."

---

## Stage 3 - Exit: AUM Conversion Event

The exit is the financial event that converts
the co-investment into an AUM growth opportunity.

**Direct AUM conversion:**
Exit proceeds received by the co-investing client
are liquid capital seeking redeployment. The
client's existing relationship with Meridian
makes the private banking mandate the natural
home for these proceeds. A client who receives
USD 2M in exit proceeds and adds them to their
existing USD 10M discretionary mandate has
increased Meridian's AUM by 20% from a single
exit event. The fee revenue impact is immediate
and recurring.

**Deepened relationship at redeployment:**
The exit redeployment conversation is a full
portfolio review moment. The client has received
a significant liquidity event and is making a
meaningful asset allocation decision. This is
the highest-value advisory interaction available
to a private banking relationship manager. The
quality of this conversation - whether the
relationship manager brings genuine portfolio
construction insight or simply routes the capital
into the existing mandate - determines whether
the client relationship deepens or stays static.

**Referral conversion:**
Exit events with strong returns generate referral
conversations. A client who has made 4x on a
co-investment is a motivated advocate for Meridian's
investment approach. The relationship manager's
role is to identify the specific individuals in
the client's network who might benefit from a
Meridian relationship and to facilitate warm
introductions. One exit event in a strong co-investment
can generate two or three new client introductions
from a single satisfied co-investor.

---

## The Compounding Logic

The AUM conversion flywheel compounds because
each successful co-investment event:

Increases current AUM from exit proceed redeployment.
Increases referral probability which increases
future AUM from new client acquisition. Increases
the co-investment track record which increases
the attractiveness of future co-investment offerings
to the existing eligible client base.

Each cycle of the flywheel produces more AUM,
more referrals, and more co-investment demand
than the previous cycle. The compounding rate
depends on exit quality and exit frequency.
A fund with strong returns and regular exit events
generates a compounding AUM growth effect from
the co-investment architecture that a fund with
the same returns but fewer client touchpoints
does not produce.

## Quantitative Illustration

**Assumptions for illustration only:**
Fund I deploys USD 200M across 12 positions.
Average holding period 5 to 7 years.
Base case gross MOIC 2.95x on invested capital.
Co-investment tranche per deal average 15% of Meridian position.
Average co-investment per eligible client USD 400K.
Number of co-investing clients across Fund I deals 120.
Average exit proceeds per co-investing client USD 1.18M
(USD 400K x 2.95x gross).
AUM conversion rate from exit proceeds 70%
(70% of exit proceeds reinvested into Meridian mandate).
Average AUM per converted proceed USD 826K.

**AUM conversion from co-investment exits:**
120 co-investing clients x USD 826K average AUM conversion
= USD 99.1M in incremental AUM from co-investment
exit conversions over the fund lifecycle.

At 100 basis points average fee revenue on AUM,
USD 99.1M in incremental AUM generates USD 991K
in incremental annual fee revenue on a recurring basis.

The venture fund generates carry. The co-investment
architecture generates recurring fee revenue growth.
The two streams are complementary, not additive.
The combined return to the Meridian institution
from a successful venture fund exceeds what either
the carry alone or the AUM growth alone would produce.
