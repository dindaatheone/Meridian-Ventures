# Co-Investment Mechanics - Meridian Ventures

## The Co-Investment Architecture

Co-investment in Meridian Ventures is not a client
benefit add-on. It is a structural mechanism that
converts venture fund returns into private banking
AUM growth events and client relationship deepening
simultaneously. Every co-investment event serves
three functions: it provides additional capital
to a portfolio company at a critical growth stage,
it creates direct financial alignment between
Meridian's private banking clients and the venture
portfolio, and it produces an exit event whose
proceeds flow back into the wealth management
relationship as discretionary AUM.

---

## Eligibility Framework

### Client Eligibility

Co-investment access is extended to clients with
the co_investment_eligible_flag set to TRUE in
the Meridian-Business-Intelligence client entity
schema. This flag is set at VHNW and UHNW tiers
only. HNW clients do not receive co-investment
access because the minimum ticket size required
for meaningful co-investment economics exceeds
what is appropriate for clients at the USD 1M
to 5M AUM level.

Within the eligible client base, co-investment
opportunities are further filtered by sector
relevance and risk appetite. A Brunei-based UHNW
client with a conservative risk appetite and a
primary interest in Islamic finance would not be
offered a co-investment in a Macau-based real
estate analytics platform. The co-investment
offering is matched to client profile with the
same rigor applied to portfolio mandate construction.

### Minimum Ticket Size

USD 250K minimum per co-investment opportunity.
This floor exists for three reasons.

First, below USD 250K, the legal and administrative
cost of onboarding a co-investor onto the cap table
exceeds the relationship value created. The Meridian
private banking relationship manager time required
to explain the investment, document the client's
suitability assessment, process the subscription
documents, and manage the ongoing co-investor
communication is a real cost that must be justified
by the ticket size.

Second, below USD 250K, the position is too small
to be meaningful in the client's portfolio. A
USD 5M VHNW client investing USD 100K in a venture
co-investment has taken on binary risk for 2% of
their portfolio. That allocation profile is not
consistent with sophisticated wealth management practice.

Third, USD 250K represents the minimum position
at which the client will engage seriously with
the co-investment opportunity rather than treating
it as a minor speculative allocation. Serious
engagement - reading the diligence materials,
understanding the investment thesis, monitoring
the portfolio company's progress - is what creates
the relationship deepening value that makes
co-investment strategically valuable to Meridian.

## Allocation Process

### Step 1 - IC Approval and Co-Investment Designation

At the time of IC approval, the investment committee
designates whether the deal is suitable for
co-investment offering and determines the maximum
co-investment tranche size. The maximum is 20%
of the total deal size across Meridian fund capital,
LP advisory board co-investment, and client co-investment
combined.

For a USD 10M Meridian investment where Meridian
leads a USD 50M post-money valuation round, the
total co-investment tranche available to Meridian
to allocate is USD 10M x 20% = USD 2M.

### Step 2 - Client Notification

Within 30 days of IC approval, eligible private
banking clients are notified of the co-investment
opportunity through their assigned relationship
manager. The notification includes a co-investment
summary document that presents the investment thesis,
the key diligence findings, the proposed terms,
and the risk factors in plain language appropriate
for a sophisticated non-professional investor.

The co-investment summary is prepared by the
Investment Director and reviewed by the Managing
Partner before distribution. It does not reproduce
the full IC submission package, which contains
competitively sensitive information. It provides
the information a sophisticated investor needs
to make an informed decision about allocating
personal capital.

### Step 3 - Subscription and Allocation

Eligible clients who wish to participate submit
subscription documents within 15 days of notification.
Oversubscription is allocated pro-rata based on
the client's total investable AUM at Meridian.
Larger clients receive proportionally larger
co-investment allocations in oversubscribed situations.

Undersubscription - where client demand does not
fill the designated co-investment tranche - is
not an adverse signal. Many clients will not
participate in any given co-investment opportunity
for legitimate portfolio construction reasons.
Undersubscribed tranches are offered to LP advisory
board members before any co-investment allocation
is returned to the Meridian fund.

### Step 4 - Ongoing Communication

Co-investors receive the same quarterly portfolio
company updates that the Meridian fund receives,
redacted for competitively sensitive information.
This communication serves two purposes: it keeps
co-investors informed about their investment, and
it creates regular touchpoints between the private
banking client and Meridian that reinforce the
institutional relationship.

## Co-Investment at Exit

The exit event is the highest-value moment in the
co-investment relationship lifecycle. A client who
co-invested USD 500K alongside Meridian in a
portfolio company that exits at a 4x gross MOIC
receives USD 2M in exit proceeds. That USD 2M
is not a one-time windfall. It is a relationship
moment.

The relationship manager's role at exit is to
engage the client on three questions: How does
the exit proceeds allocation fit into the client's
broader portfolio? What is the client's current
thinking about reinvestment of the exit proceeds
into the discretionary or advisory mandate? Are
there other family members or associates who
experienced the client's co-investment success
and who might be interested in establishing their
own private banking relationship with Meridian?

The exit conversation, handled correctly, converts
a venture return into an AUM growth event and a
client referral opportunity simultaneously.
