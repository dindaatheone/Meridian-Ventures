# Deal Sourcing Network - Meridian Ventures

## The Sourcing Moat

Deal sourcing quality is the primary determinant
of venture fund returns at the portfolio level.
A fund with access to the full population of
fundable companies in its target market and the
ability to identify the strongest opportunities
within that population before competitive processes
begin generates returns that a fund dependent on
intermediary introductions and competitive auction
processes cannot replicate, regardless of analytical
sophistication.

Meridian's sourcing moat derives from a single
structural fact: the private banking client relationships
that are the core of the wealth management business
are simultaneously the most productive deal sourcing
network available for Asia-Pacific venture opportunities
in the five target sectors.

This is not a coincidence of geography. It is a
consequence of the specific client profiles that
Meridian's boutique positioning attracts.

## How the Network Generates Deal Flow

### Channel 1 - Family Business Succession Events

UHNW clients across the five corridors who are
managing family business transitions are among
the most productive sources of venture deal flow
in Asia-Pacific private banking.

A Chinese manufacturing family office client whose
second-generation members are building a wealthtech
startup generates deal access unavailable to any
generalist fund. The private banking relationship
creates trust that the business relationship requires.
The family is not approaching venture funds cold.
They are sharing an opportunity with a banker whose
judgment they trust with their wealth.

The same pattern applies across all five corridors.
Indonesian conglomerate families with third-generation
members building Islamic finance technology platforms.
Brunei sovereign-adjacent families whose younger
members are creating family office operational
software. Macau private wealth families restructuring
holding company assets into operating ventures.
In every case, the private banking relationship
is the sourcing channel that produces the deal.

### Channel 2 - Portfolio Company Referral Network

Each Meridian Ventures portfolio company becomes
a node in a compounding deal referral network.
Founders refer founders. A portfolio company CEO
who has experienced Meridian's value-added approach
to board engagement, co-investment structuring,
and cross-corridor market intelligence refers
the next generation of founders from their own
network to Meridian rather than to generalist funds.

This channel grows with every successful portfolio
company addition. The first five positions build
a referral network. The next five benefit from it.
By year five of the deployment period, a meaningful
proportion of new deal flow arrives through referrals
from existing portfolio company founders.

### Channel 3 - Sector Expert Network Activation

Meridian's private banking client base across five
corridors contains former executives and current
operators in every one of the five target sectors.
A Singapore-based private banking client who was
previously the CTO of a major Asia-Pacific financial
data infrastructure company is a more valuable
source of diligence intelligence on a wealthtech
investment opportunity than any market research
report.

These sector experts are not paid advisors or formal
network members. They are private banking clients
whose relationship with Meridian creates a natural
willingness to share market intelligence in the
context of investment conversations. This channel
requires no formal structure. It requires only
that the private banking relationship managers
who serve these clients understand which clients
have relevant sector expertise and create the
connection when a relevant investment opportunity
is in diligence.

### Channel 4 - Regulatory Intelligence Network

Meridian's multi-corridor private banking operations
generate continuous regulatory intelligence across
Singapore MAS, Indonesia OJK, Brunei financial
regulators, Hong Kong SFC, and the relevant Islamic
finance certification bodies. This regulatory
intelligence creates deal sourcing advantage in
two ways.

First, regulatory change events consistently produce
venture opportunities as the compliance requirements
that regulation creates generate demand for new
technology solutions. Meridian's regulatory intelligence
positions the fund to identify these opportunities
before they become visible to generalist funds.

Second, regulatory risk assessment during diligence
benefits from the ground-level intelligence that
comes from operating across five regulatory environments
simultaneously. A fund that operates only in Singapore
has significantly less insight into the regulatory
risk of an Indonesian OJK-regulated portfolio company
than a fund whose private banking operations require
daily engagement with Indonesian regulatory requirements.

## Sourcing Discipline

Proprietary deal flow is a necessary but insufficient
condition for strong venture returns. A fund with
access to every deal in the market but no ability
to distinguish the best opportunities from the
rest generates mediocre returns from a privileged
sourcing position.

Meridian's sourcing discipline operates through
three filters applied before any opportunity reaches
the formal diligence process.

**Filter 1 - Mandate fit assessment:**
Does the opportunity fall within the five sectors
and five corridors defined in the blind pool thesis?
Off-mandate opportunities, regardless of quality,
are declined at the sourcing stage. The integrity
of the mandate is more valuable than any individual
opportunity.

**Filter 2 - Stage gate pre-assessment:**
Has the company accumulated enough execution evidence
to clear the Stage Gate 1 mandatory criteria? Companies
that clearly cannot meet the revenue threshold, team
assessment, or unit economics requirements are declined
at the sourcing stage rather than consuming diligence
resources. The pre-assessment is conducted by the
Investment Director based on publicly available
information and the sourcing channel intelligence.

**Filter 3 - Strategic fit pre-assessment:**
Does the opportunity create co-investment suitability
for Meridian's private banking client base? Does
the founding team network add to Meridian's sourcing
coverage? Is there private banking intelligence
value in the investment? Opportunities that clear
filters 1 and 2 but provide no strategic fit value
are deprioritized in favor of opportunities that
clear all three filters.
