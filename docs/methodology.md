# Methodology - Meridian Ventures

Complete documentation of the analytical approach,
architectural decisions, and theoretical foundations
governing Meridian Ventures Fund I. Every decision
documented here traces back to a specific analytical
or institutional rationale.

---

## Why the Hybrid Architecture

The hybrid decision - PE fund architecture nested
inside private banking integration logic - was
reached by evaluating what each pure structure
produces and what it fails to produce.

A pure PE fund produces institutional credibility
through its GP/LP structure, analytical rigor through
its IC framework, and LP alignment through its
waterfall mechanics. What it cannot produce is
proprietary deal flow from a UHNW client network,
co-investment distribution through an existing
wealth management client base, or due diligence
intelligence from private banking operational
experience in the target market.

A pure private banking venture arm produces client
relationship deepening through co-investment access
and proprietary deal flow through existing client
networks. What it cannot produce is the institutional
credibility required to attract blind pool commitment
from sophisticated LP investors who evaluate fund
governance by the quality of its formal investment
infrastructure.

The hybrid produces all six outcomes simultaneously.
The cost of the hybrid is the organizational
complexity of operating two institutional frameworks
simultaneously. The benefit is a competitive
position that neither pure structure can achieve
or replicate.

## Why Real Options Governs Capital Deployment

The Bhide finding - 88% of successful venture founders
attribute success to exceptional execution of ordinary
ideas - establishes execution quality as the primary
determinant of venture returns. Real options theory
establishes staged capital deployment as the value-
maximizing response to uncertainty about execution quality.

The two frameworks are complementary in a specific
way: Bhide tells us what to look for (execution evidence),
and real options tells us how to structure the capital
deployment process given that the evidence accumulates
over time rather than being available at a single moment.

Series A capital is deployed when execution evidence
has reduced subjective uncertainty enough for institutional
analysis to replace speculation, but before execution
quality is fully priced. Series B follow-on is deployed
when Series A execution evidence has validated the
hypothesis and the compound option value of participating
before the growth equity market fully prices the evidence
remains positive. Abandonment is triggered when execution
evidence has falsified the hypothesis irreversibly.

At each decision point, the IC is exercising one of
four real options: the defer option at Series A entry,
the expand option at Series B follow-on, the abandon
option at the Stage Gate 3 evaluation, and the expand
option again at above pro-rata follow-on deployment.

## Why Series A to B is the Target Window

Three structural characteristics make Series A to B
the correct target window for Meridian's capital.

First, the financing sequence creates a natural filter.
Pre-seed and seed capital has already funded the
uncertainty reduction phase. By Series A, angels and
early-stage funds have produced enough execution evidence
to evaluate. Meridian benefits from the information
generated by earlier capital without bearing the
information generation risk.

Second, the co-investment opportunity set is richest
at this stage. The minimum ticket size for meaningful
co-investment - USD 250K - is appropriate for VHNW
and UHNW clients at this stage. Earlier stage co-investment
either requires smaller tickets that do not generate
meaningful relationship value or exposes clients to
subjective uncertainty that is inappropriate for
private banking client risk profiles.

Third, the Expanded NPV framework is most favorable
at this stage. The compound option value of Series A
participation buying the right to follow-on at Series B
before the growth equity market prices the execution
evidence is substantial. Once Series B closes publicly,
the option value collapses to its intrinsic value and
the Expanded NPV advantage disappears.

## Why Blind Pool Over Deal-by-Deal

Three advantages of the blind pool structure over
deal-by-deal for Meridian's specific mandate.

Speed. In competitive Asia-Pacific Series A processes,
the ability to commit within the GP's authority rather
than convening an LP advisory vote is a meaningful
negotiating advantage. The best deals in the five
target sectors attract multiple institutional investors.
Speed of decision-making is one dimension of
competitiveness that a blind pool structure enables
and a deal-by-deal structure constrains.

Thesis consistency. Deal-by-deal approval creates
the risk that LPs approve deals in favorable market
conditions and reject deals in adverse conditions
regardless of fundamental investment quality. A blind
pool mandate commits LPs to the thesis for the full
fund life, allowing the GP to invest counter-cyclically
when market conditions create the best entry valuations.

Institutional signal. Blind pool commitment from
sophisticated LPs is a stronger signal of GP credibility
than deal-by-deal approval, because it represents
confidence in the framework rather than confidence
in a specific transaction. For Meridian's target LP
base - Asia-Pacific family office investment committees
and GCC sovereign co-investment vehicles - demonstrating
that the investment framework is robust enough to
attract blind pool commitment is a meaningful credential.

## How the Three Repos Integrate

Meridian Ventures is the synthesis layer of the
three-repo architecture. The integration is not
metaphorical. It is operational.

The Meridian-Business-Intelligence client entity
schema provides the co_investment_eligible_flag
and jurisdiction fields that determine which clients
receive co-investment offering and from which corridors
deal sourcing is prioritized. Without the BI spine,
the co-investment mechanics documented in 07_pb_integration
have no operational foundation.

The Meridian-Monte-Carlo LHS valuation model provides
the P10, P50, and P90 equity values that populate
the IC decision log and the deal memo valuation
summary. Without the Monte Carlo output, the Expanded
NPV calculation in 05_valuation is a framework
without numbers. The macro stress model provides
the risk dashboard content for Section 4 of the
quarterly LP report. The historical simulation model
provides the VaR, CVaR, and hurdle probability
figures that appear in the same section.

Meridian Ventures integrates both. It is where client
intelligence becomes deal sourcing advantage, where
quantitative valuation becomes IC decision discipline,
and where risk modeling becomes LP communication.
The three repos together constitute a private banking
and venture capital institution. Each repo alone
is an analytical exercise.
