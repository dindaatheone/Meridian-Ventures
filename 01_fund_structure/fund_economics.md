# Fund Economics - Meridian Ventures Fund I

## Summary

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Target Fund Size | USD 150M to 250M |
| Management Fee | 2.0% per annum on committed capital |
| Hurdle Rate | 8.0% per annum (preferred return) |
| Carried Interest | 20% of profits above hurdle |
| Clawback | Full - GP returns excess carry if fund underperforms |
| Fund Life | 10 years from first close |
| Deployment Period | Years 1 to 5 |
| Realization Period | Years 6 to 10 |
| GP Commitment | 1% to 5% of total fund commitments |
| Exit Mechanism | Recapitalization and continuation vehicles |

---

## Management Fee

2.0% per annum on committed capital during the
deployment period (years 1 to 5). After the deployment
period, the fee steps down to 2.0% on invested capital
(capital actually deployed, not returned). This step-down
reduces the fee burden on LPs as the portfolio matures
and exits begin returning capital.

The management fee covers GP operating costs: team
compensation, legal and compliance, fund administration,
portfolio monitoring, and LP communication. It is not
profit. A fund that generates returns primarily from
management fees rather than carried interest is not
creating value for LPs.

---

## Hurdle Rate

8.0% per annum, compounded annually. LPs must receive
a full return of invested capital plus 8% annual
compounded return before any carried interest is paid
to the GP.

The 8% hurdle is the industry standard for institutional
private equity funds. It reflects the minimum return
LPs require to justify the illiquidity premium of a
10-year locked-up vehicle relative to liquid alternatives.

---

## Carried Interest

20% of net profits above the hurdle rate, paid to
the GP after the full distribution waterfall is
satisfied. Carried interest is the primary GP incentive
mechanism. A GP that generates 3x gross MOIC on a
USD 200M fund creates approximately USD 600M in
total value. After returning LP capital and the hurdle,
the GP's 20% carry on the remaining profit is the
economic outcome that GP professionals work toward.

Carried interest aligns GP and LP interests because
the GP earns nothing above the management fee until
LPs are made whole plus the hurdle. There is no
incentive to exit early at mediocre returns because
doing so eliminates the carry.

---

## Clawback

If the GP receives carried interest distributions
on early successful exits and later investments
underperform such that total fund returns fall below
the hurdle, the GP must return the excess carry
distributions to LPs. This is the clawback provision.

Clawback protects LPs against the scenario where
early portfolio company exits generate carry payments
but late exits destroy value. Without clawback, GPs
could rationally harvest early winners and abandon
underperformers, leaving LPs with net returns below
the hurdle while the GP has already collected carry.

---

## Fund Life and Lifecycle

**Year 1 to 2 - Fundraising and Initial Deployment**
First close targets 60% of total commitment. Initial
investments made from first close capital. Management
fee begins at first close.

**Year 2 to 5 - Full Deployment**
Remaining committed capital deployed across the
target portfolio of 10 to 15 positions. Follow-on
reserves allocated for Series B participation in
the strongest performers.

**Year 6 to 10 - Realization**
Portfolio companies pursued toward exit via strategic
acquisition, secondary sale, or recapitalization into
continuation vehicle. No new platform investments.
Management fee steps down to invested capital basis.
Distributions made as exits close.

**Year 10 - Fund Termination or Extension**
Fund terminates at year 10 unless LP Advisory Board
approves a one-year extension (up to two extensions)
to allow remaining positions to reach optimal exit.

---

## Exit Mechanism

Meridian Ventures Fund I does not pursue third-party
sale of the fund itself as an exit. The institutional
posture is evergreen: positions are exited via
strategic acquisition of portfolio companies,
secondary sale of fund interests to continuation
vehicles, or recapitalization events. The GP entity
persists beyond Fund I to manage Fund II and the
ongoing private banking institution.

This evergreen posture is the correct structure for
an institution that is building a long-term brand
in Asia-Pacific private banking and venture investing.
Selling the fund destroys the institutional continuity
that makes the GP valuable to future LPs and portfolio
companies.
