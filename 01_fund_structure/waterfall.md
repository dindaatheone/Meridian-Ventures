# Distribution Waterfall - Meridian Ventures Fund I

## Waterfall Order

Money flows to LPs first. Before the GP
receives any carried interest. This is the defining
feature of the LP-protective waterfall structure.

### Step 1 - Return of LP Capital

100% of all distributions go to LPs until they have
received back their full invested capital contribution.
The GP receives nothing beyond the management fee
during this step.

### Step 2 - Preferred Return (Hurdle)

100% of distributions continue to go to LPs until
they have received an 8% per annum compounded annual
return on their invested capital from the date of
each capital call. This is the preferred return
or hurdle rate.

### Step 3 - GP Catch-Up

After LPs have received capital return plus the full
8% preferred return, 100% of distributions go to
the GP until the GP has received 20% of total profits
distributed to that point. This is the catch-up
provision. It allows the GP to reach its contractual
20% carry share without requiring every distribution
to be split 80/20.

### Step 4 - Carried Interest Split

After the GP catch-up is complete, all remaining
distributions split 80% to LPs and 20% to the GP.
This continues for all subsequent distributions
until the fund is wound down.

## Worked Numerical Example

**Fund assumptions for this example:**
Total LP commitments: USD 200M
GP commitment: USD 2M (1%)
Total fund: USD 202M
Gross exit proceeds: USD 500M

**Step 1 - Return of LP Capital**
LPs receive: USD 200M
Remaining to distribute: USD 300M

**Step 2 - Preferred Return Calculation**
Assume 5-year average holding period for capital.
8% compounded over 5 years on USD 200M:
USD 200M x (1.08^5 - 1) = USD 200M x 0.469 = USD 93.9M
LPs receive: USD 93.9M
Remaining to distribute: USD 206.1M

**Step 3 - GP Catch-Up**
Total profits distributed so far: USD 93.9M
GP is entitled to 20% of total profits: USD 93.9M x 20/80 = USD 23.5M
GP receives: USD 23.5M in catch-up
Remaining to distribute: USD 182.6M

**Step 4 - 80/20 Split**
LPs receive: USD 182.6M x 80% = USD 146.1M
GP receives: USD 182.6M x 20% = USD 36.5M

**Final Summary:**
Total LP receipts: USD 200M + USD 93.9M + USD 146.1M = USD 440M
Total GP receipts (carry only): USD 23.5M + USD 36.5M = USD 60M
Total fund distributions: USD 500M
Gross MOIC to LPs: USD 440M / USD 200M = 2.2x
GP carry as % of gross profits: USD 60M / USD 298M = 20.1%

## J-Curve

The J-curve describes the cumulative cash flow pattern
of a private equity fund over its lifecycle. It is
shaped like the letter J and is one of the most
important concepts for LP investors to understand
before committing to an illiquid fund.

**Years 1 to 3 - The Trough**
LPs pay management fees and make capital calls for
investments. No exits have occurred. Cumulative cash
flow is negative and deepening. The portfolio is
marked at cost or below cost as early-stage companies
burn cash toward their milestones.

**Years 3 to 5 - The Bottom**
Some early investments begin showing positive marks
as Series B and growth rounds validate earlier
valuations. But no exits have closed. Cumulative
cash flow is still negative. This is the period
when LP patience is most tested.

**Years 6 to 8 - The Turn**
First exits begin returning capital. Cumulative cash
flow crosses zero and begins climbing. Each exit
accelerates the curve upward. The portfolio is
marked up based on comparable transactions and the
improving quality of exit pipeline.

**Years 8 to 10 - The Vertical**
Remaining exits close. The J-curve inflects sharply
upward. LPs who held through the trough and bottom
receive distributions that represent the full value
of the patient capital commitment.

**Meridian Ventures Fund I J-Curve Expectation:**
Trough at years 2 to 3 reflecting management fees
and initial capital calls. First distributions
targeted for years 6 to 7 from the strongest
Series A positions that have progressed to Series B
or growth stage exits. Full portfolio realization
targeted years 8 to 10.
