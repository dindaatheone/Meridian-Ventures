# GP/LP Structure - Meridian Ventures Fund I

## General Partner

**Entity:** Meridian Capital Management Pte. Ltd.
**Jurisdiction:** Singapore
**Role:** All investment decisions. Fund management.
Portfolio company oversight. LP communication.
**Capital Contribution:** 1% to 5% of total fund commitments.
**Liability:** Unlimited as GP. Full clawback obligations.
**Compensation:** 2% annual management fee on committed
capital during deployment period, transitioning to
invested capital during realization period. 20% carried
interest on profits above the 8% hurdle rate.

---

## Limited Partners

LPs are passive investors with liability limited to
their committed capital. They do not participate in
investment decisions. They receive quarterly reporting,
annual audited financial statements, and distribution
notices as exits occur.

**Target LP Profile:**

| LP Type | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Asia-Pacific Family Offices | Long time horizon. Illiquidity tolerance. Direct alignment with Meridian's client corridor. Potential for co-investment alongside the fund. |
| GCC Sovereign Co-Investment Vehicles | Institutional sophistication. Large ticket size. Cross-corridor relationship value for private banking side. |
| Select University Endowments | Perpetual capital. Proven blind pool commitment history. Reputational anchor for Fund I. No quarterly liquidity requirement. |

**What LPs Are Not:**
Retail investors. Short-horizon institutional allocators.
Fund of funds seeking liquidity within 3 years. LPs
who require deal-by-deal approval rights. These LP
profiles are structurally incompatible with the blind
pool model and the 10-year fund life.

---

## Blind Pool Structure

Meridian Ventures Fund I operates as a blind pool.
LPs commit capital at fund close before any specific
investments have been identified. Commitment is based
on the GP's investment mandate, track record, and
institutional thesis - not on specific deal approval.

### Why Blind Pool

**Signal of institutional maturity.** Deal-by-deal
funds require LP approval for each investment. This
structure is appropriate for emerging managers with
limited track records who need LP oversight to build
trust. Blind pool funds signal that the GP's investment
framework is robust enough to attract commitment on
thesis alone. This is a higher-trust, higher-credibility
structure.

**Speed advantage.** When a deal requires commitment
within a compressed timeframe, a blind pool GP can
move without convening an LP advisory vote. In
competitive Asia-Pacific venture situations, speed
of commitment is a negotiating advantage.

**Thesis consistency.** Deal-by-deal approval creates
the risk that LPs approve early deals and reject
later deals based on market sentiment rather than
investment merit. Blind pool commitment locks in the
thesis for the full fund life, protecting portfolio
construction discipline.

### The Thesis as Credential

For a new manager without a prior fund track record,
the blind pool thesis must substitute for historical
performance. The three Meridian repos collectively
function as the institutional credential:

Meridian-Business-Intelligence proves the analytical
infrastructure exists to source and evaluate deals
with institutional rigor.

Meridian-Monte-Carlo proves the quantitative risk
framework exists to price uncertainty in illiquid
Asia-Pacific assets.

Meridian-Ventures documents the complete capital
deployment framework that governs how committed
capital is deployed.

Together these demonstrate that the institutional
infrastructure for managing LP capital responsibly
is in place before the first dollar is committed.

## Advisory Board

An LP Advisory Board comprising two to three LP
representatives provides oversight on conflicts of
interest, fund term amendments, and valuation disputes.
The Advisory Board has no investment decision authority.
It is a governance mechanism, not a co-decision body.
